## Purpose of This File

This file defines the official evolution log of the Engineering Work Doctrine.

Its purpose is to preserve the living memory of the doctrine as it changes over time.

A doctrine that evolves without memory becomes:
- unstable
- hard to interpret
- difficult to trust
- difficult to refine responsibly
- vulnerable to repeating old mistakes
- semantically inconsistent across time

This file exists to prevent that.

The doctrine must not only evolve.  
It must remember how and why it evolved.

---

## Role of the Evolution Log in the Doctrine

The Evolution Log is the historical memory of doctrinal change.

Its role is to record:

- what changed
- when it changed
- why it changed
- what layer of the doctrine was affected
- what operational consequence the change has
- what version impact the change carries
- what lesson or pattern triggered the refinement

This file is one of the doctrine's governance instruments.

Without it, the framework may still change, but its growth becomes opaque.

---

## Evolution Principle

Every meaningful evolution of the doctrine must leave a visible trace.

The doctrine rejects:
- silent improvement
- silent redefinition
- silent tightening of rules
- silent weakening of standards
- silent semantic drift

If a doctrine change matters, the doctrine must remember it.

---

## Logging Rule

A log entry should be added whenever there is a meaningful change to the doctrine, including but not limited to:

- a new document added
- a core concept clarified significantly
- a lifecycle rule adjusted
- a discovery, consolidation, readiness, or delivery rule materially refined
- a new anti-pattern identified
- a truth-status interpretation strengthened
- a quality rule hardened
- a governance behavior changed
- a new usage path added
- a version change declared
- a lesson from real-world application that affects doctrine behavior

Minor typo fixes do not always require a full doctrinal entry unless they affect interpretation.

Use judgment, but err toward visible memory when in doubt.

---

## Relationship to Versioning

The Evolution Log and the Versioning Policy work together, but they are not identical.

### Versioning Policy
Explains how changes should be classified.

### Evolution Log
Records what actually changed.

Versioning answers:
- What kind of change is this?

The Evolution Log answers:
- What changed, why, and what does it mean now?

A meaningful doctrine needs both.

### Reason for Change

The doctrine was originally built to govern only new project creation. However, the user's vision was that the doctrine should govern ANY task an AI executes — not just new projects. A deep analysis confirmed that the doctrine's lifecycle was incompatible with bugfixes, refactoring, features, small tasks, and questions. The "birth" metaphor was wrong for maintenance work. The single heavy lifecycle was wrong for small tasks. The all-or-nothing contract was wrong for tiered work.

This reconception addresses these gaps while preserving the doctrine's core strengths: discovery before construction, consolidation before execution, agreement before build, readiness before delivery, and structural quality preservation.

The Proportionality Principle is the key enabler — it allows the doctrine to be universal (applicable to all work) without being rigid (too heavy for small work) or shallow (too light for large work).

### Operational Impact
- The doctrine now governs all engineering work, not just new projects.
- The AI must classify work before processing — this is the new first action.
- Six lifecycle paths replace the single project-only lifecycle.
- Work agreements are tiered — small tasks get lightweight briefs, projects get full agreements.
- Brownfield work (existing systems) has dedicated discovery protocol.
- Small tasks and questions are no longer over-engineered with heavy process.
- Large tasks are no longer under-served by trivial process.
- The doctrine folder has been renamed from `PROJECT_BIRTH_DOCTRINE/` to `ENGINEERING_WORK_DOCTRINE/`.
- All governance documents (README, AGENTS, DECISIONS, ENVIRONMENT, OPERATIONS) have been updated.

### Migration / Interpretation Notes
Prior references to "Project Birth Doctrine", "project birth", "Project Birth Contract", and "born" in older readings should be interpreted as "Engineering Work Doctrine", "work initiation", "Work Agreement", and "initiated" respectively. The structural concepts are preserved where applicable — the change is one of scope expansion and terminology generalization, not abandonment of the original principles.

The original project-birth lifecycle is preserved as the "Full Lifecycle" path within the new multi-path system. Projects still receive the full process — the change is that other work types now receive appropriately scaled process instead of either the full process (over-engineering) or no process (under-engineering).

### Lessons / Observations
Scope limitation is a form of structural dependency. A framework that only applies to one type of work is dependent on that work type — it cannot be reused for other work without adaptation. By expanding the scope to all engineering work, the doctrine becomes truly universal.

### Summary of Change
Added the **Operational State** dimension — the third axis of the doctrine (alongside Work Type and Proportionality).

The doctrine previously assumed all work was production-grade. This created a fundamental problem: rules designed for precious output (100% as Floor, no workarounds, Rigid Payload, nothing modified without trace, first execution correct) were being applied to formative work where they actively harmed — killing iteration speed, creating unnecessary ceremony, preventing migration squashing, and treating throwaway spikes as if they were production code.

The Operational State dimension resolves this by making the rules scale with **output preciousness**:

1. **Exploratory** — output is throwaway. Process is minimal. Workarounds are tools. No Rigid Payload. No traceability. Goal: learn whether something is viable.

2. **Formative** — output is being shaped. Consolidation is encouraged (squash migrations, rewrite freely). Workarounds allowed with notes. Rigid Payload only on committed changes. 100% means "100% of current understanding." Goal: find the right shape.

3. **Stable** — output is precious. Full doctrine applies. 100% non-negotiable. No workarounds. Rigid Payload mandatory. Migrations ordered and reversible. Goal: safe evolution of live systems.

The transition from Formative to Stable is a **one-way door** called the Consolidation Moment — a deliberate ritual where migrations are squashed, workarounds are removed, tests are written, and the architecture is frozen. No work drifts into Stable.

The state is **per-item, not per-project**: a project can have a Stable core module, a Formative new feature, and an Exploratory spike all at the same time.

**Default is Stable** when the state is unclear — this preserves the doctrine's existing behavior. The user may declare a lighter state, but the AI must not assume it.

### Reason for Change
The user identified that the doctrine was "hardcoded" for a single usage scenario (production-grade work) and actively harmed in other scenarios — particularly during active development where migration squashing, free rewriting, and workaround experimentation are the correct engineering practice, not doctrinal failures.

This was not a bug or a gap — it was a **missing dimension**. The doctrine had two axes (Work Type and Proportionality) but was missing the third (Output Preciousness). Without this third axis, the doctrine could not distinguish "fix a bug in production" from "fix a bug in a prototype" — applying the same rules to both, which is wrong.

### Operational Impact
- The doctrine now correctly handles development-phase work without killing iteration
- Migration squashing is now doctrinally sanctioned during Formative state
- Workarounds are now legitimate tools during Exploratory/Formative, with tracking for cleanup
- The Rigid Payload is now required proportionally — not for throwaway or experimental changes
- The 100% criterion is refined: "100% of current understanding" during Formative, full 100% during Stable
- The Consolidation Moment provides a clear, deliberate transition from development to production-grade work
- Per-item state allows mixed-state projects (Stable core + Formative feature + Exploratory spike)
- Default-to-Stable preserves backward compatibility — existing behavior is unchanged unless a state is declared

### Lessons / Observations
This change reveals an important principle of doctrine design: **rules must scale with the consequences of being wrong**. In production, being wrong is expensive (downtime, data loss, user impact) — so the rules are strict. In development, being wrong is cheap (rewrite, squash, try again) — so the rules are relaxed. A doctrine that applies the same rules regardless of consequences is not proportionate — it is rigid.

The Operational State dimension also reveals that **traceability is not always valuable**. During formation, the history of intermediate states is noise, not signal. Squashing migrations is not destroying history — it is removing noise to reveal the signal. The doctrine previously treated all history as valuable; now it recognizes that history becomes valuable only when the output becomes precious.

The Consolidation Moment is the doctrinal answer to the question "when does development become production?" — it is not a gradual drift but a deliberate act with a ritual. This prevents two failures: never declaring Stable (endless iteration) and drifting into Stable without consolidation (messy production).
