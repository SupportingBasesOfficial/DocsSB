# CONSOLIDATION PROTOCOL

## Purpose of This File

This file defines the official consolidation protocol of the Engineering Work Doctrine.

Consolidation is the stage that transforms discovery outputs into coherent work meaning.

Discovery reveals.  
Consolidation organizes.  
Discovery surfaces dimensions.  
Consolidation gives them structural relationship.  
Discovery produces clarified material.  
Consolidation turns that material into a formal candidate for work initiation.

This file exists to prevent the following failures:

- moving from discovery directly into execution
- treating fragmented answers as if they were already coherent
- mistaking clarified fragments for formal work identity
- allowing horizon, scope, and implementation sequence to blur together
- failing to classify unresolved gaps before agreement creation
- silently converting partial understanding into apparent certainty

Consolidation is the discipline that turns discovery into work definition.

> **Proportionality Note:** Consolidation depth scales with work type. Full consolidation (described here) applies to projects. For features, refactoring, bugfixes, tasks, and questions, consolidation depth is reduced proportionally — see the relevant lifecycle path in `03_PROJECT_LIFECYCLE.md`.

> **Operational State Note:** Consolidation depth also scales with Operational State. In Stable state, full consolidation is required. In Formative state, consolidation may be lighter (the work is still being shaped). In Exploratory state, consolidation is minimal or skipped (the output is throwaway). The Consolidation Moment (Formative → Stable transition) is the most important consolidation event — see `21_OPERATIONAL_STATES.md`.

> **Mega-tech Consolidation:** In Stable state, consolidation must also verify the mega-tech landscape: testing strategy is defined (`23_TESTING_STRATEGY_PROTOCOL.md`), ADRs are recorded (`24_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION_RECORDS.md`), observability is present (`25_OBSERVABILITY_PROTOCOL.md`), security review is completed (`26_SECURITY_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md`), technical debt is tracked (`27_TECHNICAL_DEBT_PROTOCOL.md`), dependencies are evaluated (`28_DEPENDENCY_MANAGEMENT_PROTOCOL.md`), documentation is updated (`29_DOCUMENTATION_PROTOCOL.md`), incident response readiness is verified (`30_INCIDENT_RESPONSE_PROTOCOL.md`), quality gates are defined (`31_QUALITY_GATES.md`), API governance is checked (`32_API_GOVERNANCE_PROTOCOL.md`), data governance is confirmed (`33_DATA_GOVERNANCE_PROTOCOL.md`), and success metrics are defined (`34_METRICS_FEEDBACK_LOOP.md`). The Consolidation Moment ritual in `21_OPERATIONAL_STATES.md` includes all 17 steps covering these protocols.

## Role of Consolidation in the Doctrine

Within the doctrine, consolidation sits between:

- guided discovery
- and the work agreement

Its purpose is to answer the question:

**Given everything discovered so far, what does this work now mean in structured terms?**

Consolidation is where the AI must:
- synthesize discovery outputs
- distinguish what is confirmed from what is inferred
- determine what is structurally relevant
- classify what remains unresolved
- separate present need from future horizon
- transform the work into a coherent candidate for formal initiation

Without consolidation, the doctrine collapses into one of two failures:
- endless questioning with no structural synthesis
- premature construction from fragmented inputs

This stage prevents both.

---

## Consolidation Entry Condition

Consolidation begins only when discovery has produced enough structured material to support a coherent work understanding.

This means the AI must already have sufficient visibility into:

- the work's essential nature
- who it serves
- what operational flows matter
- what control layers matter
- what data implications matter
- what constraints are relevant
- what major uncertainties remain
- what structural dimensions have surfaced

If discovery still leaves these too unclear, consolidation must not begin yet.

Consolidation depends on structured discovery output.  
It does not compensate for missing discovery.

---

## Consolidation Exit Condition

Consolidation ends only when the work can be expressed as a coherent candidate for the Work Agreement.

This requires that the AI be able to clearly state:

- what the work is
- what the work is not
- what it must support
- what has been confirmed
- what remains open
- what structural implications follow from the discovery
- what plausible horizon differs from confirmed present scope
- what lacunae remain and how they are classified
- what readiness posture currently exists

If this cannot yet be stated coherently, consolidation is not complete.

---

## Core Objective of Consolidation

The core objective of consolidation is to transform discovery outputs into a coherent structural interpretation of the work.

The AI must move from:
- answers
- signals
- observations
- partial clarifications
- decision markers
- surfaced concerns

toward:
- explicit work identity
- structured meaning
- interpreted implications
- formal distinctions
- classified unknowns
- contract-ready clarity

Consolidation succeeds when the work is no longer merely clarified, but structurally intelligible.

---

## What Consolidation Must Produce

A doctrine-compliant consolidation must produce, at minimum, the following layers of meaning.

### 1. Essential Work Identity
A clear statement of what kind of work this is.

### 2. Purpose and Outcome Logic
A clear expression of what the work exists to accomplish.

### 3. Operational Nature
The class of system, tool, platform, environment, or workflow being defined.

### 4. Actor and Role Understanding
Who interacts with the system and in what capacities.

### 5. Core Flow Understanding
What must happen operationally for the work to fulfill its purpose.

### 6. Data and Record Implications
What must be stored, tracked, audited, retrieved, or protected.

### 7. Control and Governance Implications
What permissions, restrictions, approvals, histories, or accountability layers matter.

### 8. Experience Expectations
What kind of usage experience is structurally relevant.

### 9. Scale and Evolution Awareness
What plausible future dimensions matter structurally.

### 10. Constraints Map
What real-world limits or conditions shape the work.

### 11. Lacuna Map
What remains unknown, and how those unknowns are classified.

### 12. Structural Implication Summary
What architectural or systemic consequences already emerge from what is known.

These layers are the minimum substance required for a serious consolidation.

---

## Consolidation Principles

The consolidation phase must obey the following principles.

### 1. Consolidation Must Synthesize, Not Repeat
The AI must not merely restate user answers or replay discovery blocks.

It must organize them into structural meaning.

### 2. Consolidation Must Distinguish Signal from Structure
Not every answer has equal weight.

The AI must determine what is:
- foundational
- relevant but secondary
- open
- speculative
- structurally decisive

### 3. Consolidation Must Separate Confirmation from Interpretation
The AI must explicitly distinguish:
- what the user confirmed
- what the AI inferred
- what the AI recommends
- what remains uncertain

### 4. Consolidation Must Make Scope Legible
The AI must clarify what belongs to:
- confirmed present need
- structural foundation
- plausible future horizon
- possible later evolution

### 5. Consolidation Must Prepare Contract Readiness
The goal of consolidation is not abstract understanding.
It is agreement preparation.

### 6. Consolidation Must Not Become Delivery
Consolidation may reveal implications, but it must not collapse into full architecture or implementation detail.

---

## Official Consolidation Responsibilities of the AI

During consolidation, the AI is responsible for all of the following.

### 1. Interpret Discovery Outputs Structurally
Move from discovered fragments to coherent work meaning.

### 2. Identify the Work's Essential Form
Determine what this work fundamentally is, beyond loose wording.

### 3. Distinguish Core from Peripheral Elements
Separate what defines the work from what merely decorates it.

### 4. Separate Horizon from Scope
Clearly distinguish:
- plausible future horizon
- structural needs at initiation
- confirmed present scope
- future evolutive lacunae (possibilities that may emerge later)

### 5. Classify Open Items
Identify what remains unresolved and classify each important lacuna appropriately.

### 6. Surface Structural Consequences
Determine what architectural, governance, operational, or data consequences already follow from what is known.

### 7. Protect Against False Clarity
Do not present a partially understood work as if it were fully coherent.

### 8. Prepare the Ground for Formal Agreement
Ensure the work can now be expressed as a serious candidate for formal initiation.

## The Consolidation Model

The doctrine recommends that consolidation be organized through the following internal logic.

### Layer A — What the Work Is
A concise statement of work identity.

### Layer B — What the Work Must Do
The minimum operational mission of the work.

### Layer C — Who the Work Must Serve
The principal actors and user logic.

### Layer D — What Reality the Work Must Support
The operational environment, workflow class, or business/process context.

### Layer E — What Must Be Controlled
Permissions, governance, traceability, responsibility, approvals, irreversibility, or restrictions.

### Layer F — What Must Be Remembered
Data, history, records, state, audit trails, metrics, or event memory.

### Layer G — What Is Already Structurally Implied
High-level architectural consequences that now follow logically.

### Layer H — What Remains Open
The unresolved but visible parts of the work.

### Layer I — What the Work Could Plausibly Become
Future horizon awareness that should not be confused with current scope.

This model keeps consolidation ordered and agreement-oriented.

## Horizon, Foundation, Scope, and Sequence

One of the most important responsibilities of consolidation is to distinguish the four doctrinal layers.

### 1. Maximum Plausible Horizon
What the project could realistically become if it grows in complexity, usage, value, or scale.

### 2. Structural Foundation Needed at Initiation
What must already be considered in the initial work structure to avoid irresponsible initiation.

### 3. Confirmed Present Scope
What the user actually needs and confirms now.

### 4. Delivery Sequence
What should be delivered first without violating structural integrity.

If these layers are not clearly separated, consolidation has failed.

Common failures caused by poor separation include:
- future possibilities being mistaken for current scope
- current needs being reduced because the future was not considered
- implementation order being confused with scope definition
- architectural implications being treated as immediate build obligations

The AI must maintain these distinctions explicitly.

---

## Confirmed, Inferred, Recommended, Open

During consolidation, the AI must explicitly sort project elements into four truth-status classes.

### Confirmed
Elements directly supported by user clarification.

### Inferred
Elements not directly stated, but strongly suggested by the project context or discovery outputs.

### Recommended
Elements proposed by the AI as good practice or structural protection, even if not explicitly requested.

### Open
Elements that remain undefined, unresolved, or insufficiently confirmed.

This four-way distinction is essential.

Without it, the AI risks:
- overstating certainty
- hiding interpretation
- smuggling recommendation into fact
- weakening trust in the resulting contract

## Lacuna Classification During Consolidation

Consolidation must produce a visible lacuna map.

Each important unresolved issue must be classified as one of the following:

### Critical Blocking Lacuna
This missing definition prevents responsible work initiation or build start.

Examples:
- unknown primary actor model in a role-sensitive system
- undefined legal or operational boundary in a compliance-heavy platform
- missing clarification on whether offline capability is mandatory in an offline-critical context
- unresolved multi-tenant versus single-tenant distinction in a structurally decisive case

### Important Non-Blocking Lacuna
This missing definition matters, but the work can still proceed if the uncertainty is made explicit and structurally contained.

Examples:
- specific future reporting details
- exact notification logic
- secondary workflow variants
- preferences that affect ergonomics more than structural legitimacy

### Evolutive Lacuna
This missing definition belongs naturally to future evolution and should not delay work initiation.

Examples:
- advanced automation rules not needed for initial build direction
- future monetization detail in an internal-first project
- possible integrations with no immediate relevance
- optimization strategies beyond initial structural delivery

The AI must not only notice unknowns.  
It must govern them.

---

## What Consolidation Must Clarify Explicitly

Before consolidation can be considered complete, the AI must be able to state clearly:

### Work Identity
What this work fundamentally is.

### Work Function
What the work exists to make possible.

### Work Actors
Who interacts with it and in what roles.

### Work Reality
What domain, workflow, or operational environment it belongs to.

### Work Controls
What permissions, responsibilities, restrictions, or governance layers matter.

### Work Memory
What records, states, histories, or auditability are required.

### Work Expectations
What kind of experience and usability expectations matter.

### Work Constraints
What real-world limitations shape the work.

### Work Horizon
What plausible future paths matter structurally.

### Work Boundaries
What the work is explicitly not trying to be right now.

### Work Unknowns
What still remains unresolved.

### Work Implications
What structural consequences now follow from the known material.

If these cannot yet be clarified, more discovery may still be necessary.

---

## Consolidation Must Not Do the Following

Consolidation must not:

- pretend that clarified fragments are already a stable agreement
- convert open items into hidden assumptions
- overcommit to architecture too early
- introduce unnecessary implementation details
- confuse recommendation with confirmation
- erase ambiguity just to sound decisive
- inflate the work by treating all possibilities as required
- shrink the work by ignoring surfaced structural dimensions
- blur future horizon into immediate delivery scope

Consolidation must remain disciplined, interpretive, and agreement-oriented.

---

## Structural Implication Summaries

A correct consolidation should produce a summary of structural implications already visible from the known material.

These implications may include, depending on the work:
- likely need for role-based access
- likely need for auditable history
- likely need for stateful workflows
- likely need for mobile-first or offline-first logic
- likely need for multi-actor visibility controls
- likely need for strong data integrity rules
- likely need for multi-unit separation
- likely need for an operational admin layer
- likely need for event-driven or transactional consistency
- likely need for structured permissions and action traceability

These are not yet the final delivery artifacts.

They are the disciplined consequences of what is already known.

---

## Consolidation Completion Test

Before ending consolidation, the AI should be able to answer yes to all of the following:

- Can I clearly state what this work is?
- Can I distinguish what is confirmed from what is inferred?
- Can I separate current scope from future horizon?
- Can I state what the work must support operationally?
- Can I identify what must be controlled, stored, or protected?
- Can I identify the major constraints?
- Can I identify the most important unknowns?
- Have I classified the important lacunae properly?
- Can I state the major structural implications already visible?
- Is the work now coherent enough to become a formal work agreement candidate?

If the answer is no to any major portion of this set, consolidation is not complete.

## Consolidation Outputs

A correct consolidation should produce material ready to become the Work Agreement, including:

- clarified work identity
- clarified purpose
- actor map
- operational support definition
- control/governance implications
- data/memory implications
- scope boundary logic
- horizon versus present distinction
- lacuna map
- high-level structural implication map
- readiness posture summary

These outputs are not the final delivery.  
They are the formal bridge into initiation.

---

## Consolidation Failure Modes

Consolidation has failed when:

- discovery outputs are merely repeated rather than synthesized
- confirmed and inferred elements are mixed invisibly
- the work still feels fragmented after the supposed consolidation
- scope and horizon are confused
- unknowns remain invisible
- the AI sounds decisive while still being structurally unclear
- structural implications are not surfaced
- the work cannot yet be expressed as a coherent initiation candidate
- architecture starts replacing consolidation instead of waiting for the agreement
- the result looks polished but is still logically unstable

These are doctrinal consolidation failures.

---

## Consolidation Success Condition

Consolidation is successful when:

- discovery outputs have been transformed into coherent work meaning
- the work can be clearly stated as an identifiable system or initiative
- the major dimensions are structurally legible
- confirmed, inferred, recommended, and open items are properly distinguished
- horizon and scope are clearly separated
- lacunae are visible and classified
- structural implications are visible without becoming premature architecture
- the work is now ready to be expressed as a formal Work Agreement candidate

That is the official success condition of consolidation.
