# STRUCTURAL RECOMPOSITION PRINCIPLE

## Official Name

Integral Structural Recomposition Principle

Suggested Internal Acronym: PREI

---

## Purpose of This File

This file defines the official Structural Recomposition Principle of the ecosystem.

Its purpose is to govern how relevant changes must be treated across the entire ecosystem.

This principle exists to prevent new needs from being incorporated through minimal local adaptation, opportunistic grafting, improvised patching, structural workarounds, or any form of partial fit oriented only toward making it work.

Its role is to ensure that every relevant evolution fully preserves what already works and reorganizes the system as a whole, so that the new final state is native, coherent, intact, and structurally sound.

---

## Role of This Principle in the Doctrine

The Structural Recomposition Principle operates at the meta level of the ecosystem.

It does not govern a single project or a single session.

It governs how the entire ecosystem handles change, including:
- project structure
- doctrine itself
- reusable artifact mechanisms
- templates and manifests
- runtime and topology
- shared contexts
- operational flows
- birth and evolution mechanisms

This principle applies whenever a change is structurally relevant, regardless of whether it originates at the project level, the reusable artifact layer, or the doctrine level.

---

## Central Rule

Every relevant change to any project, base, structure, document, template, script, manifest, rule, flow, runtime, topology, shared context, reusable artifact mechanism, or systemic component of the ecosystem must be treated as a structural recomposition of the whole, and never as a minimal local adaptation.

---

## Operational Definition

A relevant change must not be thought of as:

- the smallest possible adjustment
- the minimum alteration needed to fit the new
- adapting the new to the old format
- a local patch to unblock quickly
- an opportunistic compatibility layer over the existing structure

A relevant change must be thought of as:

- integral reading of the current state
- explicit preservation of what already works
- explicit identification of what the new requires
- systemic impact analysis
- coordinated recomposition of the whole
- implementation of the new correct structural state

---

## Preservation Rule

Nothing that is already correct, validated, and functional may be sacrificed, degraded, disorganized, weakened, or made secondary in order to accommodate a new need.

Every new incorporation must happen without loss of the integrity of already-built value.

The system must come out of the change more coherent than it entered, never less.

---

## Incorporation Rule

The new must not be adapted to the old format merely to fit in.

The entire system must be reevaluated so that:

- the old continues to be correct
- the new enters correctly
- the final result is more intact than before
- the new form of the set looks native, not improvised

---

## Architecture Rule

The forbidden question is:

> What is the minimum change to make this work?

The mandatory question is:

> How should the entire system look after this change in order to remain correct, intact, functional, and natively coherent?

---

## Functional Preservation Rule

Whenever structural recomposition occurs, everything that is already validated and functional must be explicitly preserved.

Recomposition does not authorize loss of correct behavior.

It authorizes only:

- reorganization
- refinement
- expansion
- structural correction
- more mature integration of the new into the system

without loss of what already works.

---

## Prohibited Patterns

The following are prohibited as decision criteria:

- local patch
- improvised exception
- minimum solution just to unblock
- opportunistic adaptation of the new to the old
- structural duplication without governing rules
- layer overlay without reviewing the existing system
- maintenance of incoherence for convenience
- partial compatibility that preserves a prior structural defect
- maintenance of weak architecture solely to avoid rework
- "we will fix it later" as design justification

---

## Evolution Rule

Every relevant evolution must follow this order:

1. read the current system as a totality
2. identify what must be preserved
3. identify what must be incorporated
4. review structural impacts
5. redesign the whole
6. only then execute in a coordinated way

No important change should skip directly to execution without this path.

---

## AI Rule

The ecosystem AI must operate as:

- guardian of structural coherence
- systemic interpreter of impact
- agent of integral recomposition
- explicit protector of already-validated value

It must not act as a point resolver oriented toward minimum fit.

It must always ask internally:

- What already exists?
- What already works?
- What cannot be lost?
- What does the new truly require?
- Does this problem belong to the instance or to the system?
- What is the new correct structural state of the whole?

---

## Reusable Artifact Layer Rule

Whenever a problem is identified as a defect in a template, manifest, flow, runtime, topology, shared context, birth mechanism, evolution mechanism, or any reusable artifact of the reusable artifact layer, the correction must be escalated to the reusable artifact layer before being pushed as an isolated solution to a specific project.

When the problem is structural, the instance must not carry the correction as a local patch if the correct origin of the change is the reusable artifact layer.

---

## Doctrine Rule

Whenever a relevant new need arises, the doctrine, principles, contracts, manifests, and flows must be reevaluated as part of the complete system, and not altered in isolation for short-term convenience.

The doctrine must not be treated as static decorative text.

It must be treated as the normative architecture of the ecosystem.

---

## Runtime and Topology Rule

Changes to runtime, topology, working copy, execution clone, shared context, Git, automation, workspace, and birth mechanisms must be treated as operational architecture of the system, never as peripheral detail.

These layers directly alter the integrity of development, execution, and the AI's own operation.

---

## Project Rule

No serious project in the ecosystem should evolve through accumulated minimal adaptations.

Serious projects must evolve through integral structural recomposition with total preservation of what already works.

Whenever an important change arises, the project must be reevaluated as a living system, not as a collection of independent files.

---

## Relevance Threshold Rule

A change must be treated as relevant when it impacts any of these layers:

- project structure
- operational contracts
- runtime
- topology
- templates
- scripts
- manifests
- normative documents
- operational flows
- validated behavior
- AI development experience
- birth or evolution mechanisms

When any of these layers is affected, PREI applies in full.

---

## Structural Maximization Rule

Whenever more than one path exists to incorporate a new need, the decision must not seek the minimum viable path.

The decision must seek the maximum structural path that correctly preserves what already exists and correctly incorporates what is coming in.

Only when that maximum path cannot be realized may a lower fallback be considered.

Even so, the fallback must continue to obey the logic of maximum possible integrity.

---

## Success Condition

This principle is being applied correctly when:

- no relevant change is resolved through local patching
- every important change begins with an integral reading of the current state
- what already works is explicitly preserved in every recomposition
- the new enters the system natively and not as a forced fit
- the final state is more coherent than the state that preceded it
- the AI acts as a systemic guardian, not a point solver
- reusable-artifact-layer problems are corrected at the reusable artifact layer, not at the instance
- the doctrine and its artifacts are treated as normative architecture, not as decoration

That is the official success condition of the Structural Recomposition Principle.

---

## Final Rule

In the ecosystem, an important change is not adaptation.

An important change is integral structural recomposition with total preservation of already-built value.

The final state must always look native, correct, and structurally professional, as if it had been conceived that way from the beginning.
