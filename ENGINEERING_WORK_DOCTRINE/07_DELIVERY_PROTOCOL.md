# DELIVERY PROTOCOL

## Purpose of This File

This file defines the official delivery protocol of the Engineering Work Doctrine.

Delivery is the phase in which the AI begins serious, build-relevant output after the work has passed through:
- discovery
- consolidation
- work agreement formation
- build readiness verification

> **Proportionality Note:** Delivery depth scales with work type. Full structural delivery (described here) applies to projects. For features, refactoring, bugfixes, tasks, and questions, delivery depth and formality are reduced proportionally — see the relevant lifecycle path in `03_PROJECT_LIFECYCLE.md`.

This file exists to ensure that delivery:
- begins only after legitimate readiness
- remains aligned with the work's formal initiation logic
- produces implementation-relevant structure
- does not degrade into rhetorical performance
- does not overreach beyond confirmed maturity
- does not collapse structural seriousness into shallow execution
- remains proportionate, explicit, and usable

Delivery is not the first intelligent-looking answer.  
Delivery is the first serious engineering-oriented output that the work has legitimately earned.

---

## Role of Delivery in the Doctrine

Within the doctrine, delivery is the phase that begins after readiness has been explicitly granted.

Its role is to convert the work's formal initiation into structured build-oriented direction.

Delivery is where the AI must begin producing, as relevant to the work:

- executive framing
- architecture
- domain structure
- modules
- actor logic
- flow structure
- data model implications
- control and permission structures
- environment strategy
- operational sequencing
- implementation order
- technical constraints
- build artifacts where appropriate

Delivery is not discovery.  
Delivery is not consolidation.  
Delivery is not contract formation.  
Delivery is the beginning of serious work construction logic.

---

## Delivery Entry Condition

Delivery may begin only when all of the following are true:

1. discovery has been performed sufficiently
2. consolidation has produced coherent work meaning
3. a Work Agreement exists or is clearly formable
4. build readiness has been explicitly granted
5. critical blocking lacunae are absent or resolved
6. remaining uncertainty is visible and governed
7. the work has legitimate structural direction

If these conditions do not hold, delivery must not begin.

This rule is mandatory.

---

## Delivery Exit Condition

There is no single universal exit condition for delivery, because delivery may continue across multiple implementation-oriented cycles.

However, each delivery segment must end with one or more of the following:

- a coherent architectural or structural output
- an implementation-relevant artifact
- a clear next technical step
- a bounded unresolved area explicitly marked for later clarification
- a stable handoff point for continued work

Delivery fails when it produces material that sounds strong but cannot sustain implementation.

---

## Core Objective of Delivery

The core objective of delivery is to produce structured, explicit, work-specific output that can support real implementation without violating doctrinal integrity.

This means delivery must:
- honor the agreement
- respect readiness boundaries
- remain assumption-aware
- remain proportional to confirmed need
- retain structural seriousness
- give the work usable technical direction

Delivery succeeds when the work receives outputs that can be used to build responsibly.

---

## Delivery Principles

The delivery phase must obey the following principles.

### 1. Delivery Must Be Agreement-Aligned
Delivery must remain traceable to the work agreement.

The AI must not silently shift the work into a different identity during delivery.

### 2. Delivery Must Be Build-Relevant
Outputs must support implementation, not merely discussion.

### 3. Delivery Must Be Explicit
The AI must make clear:
- what is being defined
- why it is being defined
- what status that definition has
- what remains open
- what follows next

### 4. Delivery Must Be Structured
The AI must not dump ideas loosely.  
Delivery must be organized into meaningful layers or sections.

### 5. Delivery Must Be Proportionate
The AI must not underdeliver due to timidity, nor overdeliver beyond what readiness justifies.

### 6. Delivery Must Preserve Truthfulness
The AI must continue to distinguish:
- confirmed
- inferred
- recommended
- open

### 7. Delivery Must Not Perform Intelligence
Outputs must be useful for work construction, not merely impressive in tone.

---

## What Delivery Is Allowed to Produce

Once readiness is granted, delivery may include, depending on the work:

### A. Executive Framing
A concise explanation of what is being built and why the chosen structure makes sense.

### B. Structural Architecture
A clear architecture model, system shape, or major component structure.

### C. Domain Breakdown
The main business or operational domains of the work.

### D. Actor and Permission Model
The major user types, roles, access logic, and control boundaries.

### E. Flow Definitions
The key operational flows the system must support.

### F. Data Model Direction
Entities, records, states, event logic, consistency concerns, or model boundaries.

### G. Security and Governance Posture
Authentication, authorization, auditability, restrictions, approval logic, secrets handling, and control integrity where applicable.

### H. Codebase or Module Structure
Folder structure, module boundaries, service boundaries, code organization, or system layering.

### I. Environment Strategy
Dev/prod separation, deployment concerns, offline/online strategy, ownership model, runtime environments, or infrastructure posture.

### J. Build Sequencing
A technically sane order in which implementation should proceed.

### K. Concrete Artifacts
When appropriate and justified by readiness:
- migrations
- seeds
- file structures
- contracts
- schema drafts
- endpoint maps
- API structures
- full files
- implementation-ready definitions

What delivery produces depends on the work and the current maturity of the work.

---

## What Delivery Must Not Do

Delivery must not:

- begin without readiness
- contradict the work agreement silently
- overcommit beyond what has been justified
- smuggle unresolved assumptions into fixed design
- confuse recommendations with confirmed work truth
- flatten the work into generic output
- inflate the work with unnecessary sophistication
- reduce the work into shallow defaults
- replace sequencing with noise
- produce implementation material that depends on invisible premises
- prioritize rhetorical polish over technical usefulness

These are doctrinal delivery violations.

## Required Delivery Structure

Doctrine-compliant delivery should normally be organized into structured sections.

Depending on the nature of the work, these sections may include:

### 1. Executive Reading
A brief but meaningful explanation of the delivery direction.

### 2. Structural Decision Summary
What key structural decisions are being made.

### 3. Architecture
How the system is shaped at a high level.

### 4. Domains and Modules
What major parts exist and why.

### 5. Actors and Permissions
Who interacts with the system and under what boundaries.

### 6. Operational Flows
What core flows the system must support.

### 7. Data and Records
What information architecture or persistence logic matters.

### 8. Security and Governance
What protection, control, audit, and access rules are relevant.

### 9. Delivery Artifacts
Concrete outputs that can directly support implementation.

### 10. Implementation Sequence
What should be built first, second, and later.

### 11. Open Items and Reservations
What remains unresolved, if any, and how it is being governed.

The exact structure may vary, but delivery must remain legible and build-oriented.

---

## Delivery Must Preserve Layer Distinction

Even during delivery, the AI must continue to distinguish:

### Maximum Plausible Horizon
What the work could become later.

### Structural Initiation Foundation
What had to be considered from the start.

### Confirmed Current Scope
What is actually being delivered for the current work reality.

### Delivery Sequence
What is being implemented first versus later.

These four layers remain essential.

Delivery fails if the AI:
- confuses future potential with current implementation
- loses structural foundation during sequencing
- presents build order as though it were scope definition
- reduces the work simply because not everything is built at once

The doctrine allows phased implementation, but not structurally blind phasing.

---

## Delivery and Truth Status

Every serious delivery should remain clear about truth status.

The AI must continue to signal, where relevant:

### Confirmed
What follows directly from the user's clarified and consolidated work definition.

### Inferred
What is not directly stated, but strongly implied by the work's structure.

### Recommended
What the AI proposes as best practice or structural protection.

### Open
What still remains unresolved or intentionally reserved.

This truth-status discipline protects delivery from false authority.

---

## Delivery Depth Calibration

Delivery depth must be calibrated based on:

- work complexity
- work criticality
- actor complexity
- governance sensitivity
- data seriousness
- operational importance
- user constraints
- readiness maturity
- implementation urgency
- size of the justified scope

The doctrine rejects both extremes:
- shallow delivery that avoids necessary detail
- excessive delivery that runs ahead of justified clarity

The correct standard is:
**maximum seriousness, proportional depth.**

---

## Delivery Modes

Delivery may occur in different modes depending on the work state.

### Mode 1 — Structural Delivery
Used when the work needs architecture, domains, system shape, or foundational direction.

### Mode 2 — Data Delivery
Used when the work is ready for model, record, integrity, or migration-level definition.

### Mode 3 — Flow Delivery
Used when operational journeys, transitions, responsibilities, or workflow sequencing need formalization.

### Mode 4 — Codebase Delivery
Used when file structure, modules, folders, services, interfaces, or implementation scaffolding are needed.

### Mode 5 — Execution Delivery
Used when the AI begins producing concrete build artifacts ready for use.

A work item may move across multiple delivery modes over time.

The AI must choose the correct mode based on readiness and current task.

---

## Delivery Must Produce Direction, Not Just Material

The doctrine requires more than output volume.

Delivery must also provide direction.

That means every meaningful delivery should clarify:

- what has now been defined
- what this definition enables
- what should happen next
- what should not be done yet
- what remains open
- what the implementation order should be

A pile of technical material without direction is not doctrine-compliant delivery.

The AI must orient the build process, not merely add content to it.

---

## Delivery and Reservations

Not all delivery begins with perfect completeness.

If a work item is conditionally ready, delivery may proceed with reservations.

In such cases, the AI must explicitly state:

- what the reservation is
- why it does not block delivery
- what parts of delivery it limits
- what future clarification will still be required

This prevents conditional readiness from being silently converted into false completeness.

---

## Delivery Failure Modes

Delivery has failed when:

- it starts before readiness
- it ignores the work agreement
- it sounds polished but is not implementation-relevant
- it overreaches beyond confirmed maturity
- it smuggles open items into fixed design
- it becomes generic despite a specific work item
- it loses actor, flow, or governance seriousness
- it produces artifacts detached from the real work shape
- it lacks sequencing
- it hides what remains open
- it collapses into performance theater

These are doctrinal delivery failures.

## Deprecation Protocol

When work involves removing or replacing existing functionality (features, APIs, endpoints, modules), the delivery must include a **deprecation plan**. Removing functionality without a deprecation plan is a doctrinal failure in Stable state.

### When Deprecation Applies

Deprecation protocol applies when the work:
- removes an API endpoint or changes its contract
- removes a feature that users may depend on
- replaces a module whose interface is used by other parts of the system
- removes a database table, column, or data structure that may be referenced
- replaces a configuration option or behavior that users may rely on

### Deprecation Levels

| Level | Description | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft Deprecation** | Feature is marked as deprecated but still functional | Add deprecation notice in documentation and logs; warn users |
| **Hard Deprecation** | Feature is marked as deprecated with a removal date | Add deprecation warnings with timeline; provide migration path |
| **Removal** | Feature is removed | Ensure all consumers have migrated; remove code, data, infrastructure |

### Deprecation Plan Requirements

For any deprecation in Stable state, the Rigid Payload's Alterações section must include:

1. **What is being deprecated** — the specific feature, API, endpoint, or module
2. **Why it is being deprecated** — the reason (replaced, obsolete, security, performance)
3. **What replaces it** — the replacement, if any, with migration path
4. **Migration path** — how consumers migrate from the old to the new (code examples, documentation)
5. **Deprecation timeline** — when soft deprecation starts, when hard deprecation starts, when removal occurs
6. **Consumer impact analysis** — who is affected, how many consumers, what breaks
7. **Backward compatibility period** — how long the old functionality remains available during transition
8. **Communication plan** — how consumers are notified (changelog, deprecation notices, direct communication)

### Deprecation in Formative State

In Formative state, deprecation is lighter — the shape is still being discovered, so removing functionality is part of normal iteration. However, if the work item being deprecated is itself in Stable state (per-item state), the full deprecation protocol applies.

### Deprecation Anti-Patterns

- **Silent removal** — removing functionality without warning or migration path
- **Breaking change without versioning** — changing an API contract without versioning
- **No migration path** — deprecating without telling consumers how to migrate
- **Instant removal** — removing without a deprecation period (unless it's a security fix)

## Delivery Success Condition

Delivery is successful when:

- it begins only after legitimate readiness
- it remains aligned with the work agreement
- it produces structured, explicit, implementation-relevant direction
- it respects the distinction between scope, horizon, foundation, and sequence
- it remains honest about assumptions and open items
- it gives the work real technical momentum without violating structural integrity
- it produces outputs usable for serious implementation

That is the official success condition of delivery.

## Execution Flow — The 5-Stage Protocol

Once delivery is authorized, the AI must execute the work through a mandatory 5-stage flow. No stage may be skipped.

This flow is the concrete execution protocol within Stage 5 (Structured Delivery) of the lifecycle. It defines **how** to execute with mastery — not just **what** to deliver. The execution flow applies to all implementation paths (Full, Feature, Change, Fix, Light) at proportional depth. See `20_ENFORCEMENT_LAYER.md` for the five-layer architecture and `09_OUTPUT_QUALITY_STANDARD.md` for the 100% criteria and Rigid Payload format.

### Stage 1 — ENTENDER (Understand)

Before any execution, the AI must understand:
- the real objective (not just the surface request)
- the complete context (what exists, what constraints apply)
- the invariants (what must not break)
- the 100% criterion (what defines "done" for this specific work)

If any of these is unclear, the AI must clarify before proceeding. Execution without understanding is a doctrinal failure.

### Stage 2 — ESTUDAR (Study)

The AI must verify that:
- the base supports the intended change
- latent causes are identified (not just visible symptoms)
- the architecture is the most direct path (not just a workable path)
- all dependencies and side effects are mapped

**80% of effort goes here.** Study is where mastery is built. The AI must study until it knows — "I don't know" is part of ESTUDAR, not part of the final output.

Workarounds are prohibited at this stage in Stable state. If the direct solution seems hard, understanding is incomplete. Study more. (In Exploratory/Formative, workarounds are allowed with notes — see `21_OPERATIONAL_STATES.md`.)

### Stage 3 — PLANEJAR (Plan)

The AI must define:
- the most direct path to the solution
- each step, in order
- why each step is inevitable (not optional)
- what will be verified at each step

The plan must be direct — no unnecessary steps, no defensive complexity, no workarounds disguised as caution.

### Stage 4 — EXECUTAR (Execute)

The AI must execute:
- directly (no tangents)
- precisely (no approximations)
- efficiently (no wasted effort)
- perfectly (the first execution must be the correct one)

Tentativa-e-erro (trial and error) does not reach the rastro (trail). The first execution must be the correct one. If it is not, return to ESTUDAR.

### Stage 5 — VERIFICAR (Verify)

The AI must verify:
- Does the work meet 100% of the acceptance criterion?
- Is the base more solid than before?
- Does the rastro (trail) demonstrate wisdom — knowing, precision, mastery, vision?

**In Stable state**, VERIFICAR also includes the mega-tech verification protocols:
- **Testing** — all required test types pass (`23_TESTING_STRATEGY_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **ADRs** — significant decisions recorded (`24_ARCHITECTURE_DECISION_RECORDS.md`)
- **Observability** — logs, metrics, traces present (`25_OBSERVABILITY_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Security review** — completed at the appropriate level (`26_SECURITY_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Technical debt** — no untracked debt, existing debt registered (`27_TECHNICAL_DEBT_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Dependencies** — evaluated and pinned (`28_DEPENDENCY_MANAGEMENT_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Documentation** — updated and listed in Rigid Payload (`29_DOCUMENTATION_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Incident response** — rollback plan verified, response procedure documented (`30_INCIDENT_RESPONSE_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Quality gates** — all applicable gates pass (`31_QUALITY_GATES.md`)
- **API governance** — API changes reviewed (`32_API_GOVERNANCE_PROTOCOL.md`, if touching APIs)
- **Data governance** — data classified and handled (`33_DATA_GOVERNANCE_PROTOCOL.md`)
- **Metrics** — success metrics defined and collection in place (`34_METRICS_FEEDBACK_LOOP.md`)

In Formative state, these are lighter (see each protocol's state requirements). In Exploratory state, these are not required.

If the answer to any of these is no, the work is not done. Return to the appropriate stage.

### Execution Flow Principles

1. **No stage may be skipped.** Fast progression is allowed. Invisible omission is not.
2. **80/20:** 80% of effort in ENTENDER + ESTUDAR. 20% in PLANEJAR + EXECUTAR.
3. **First execution is the correct one.** Trial and error is not the path.
4. **Workarounds are prohibited in Stable state.** If the direct path seems hard, study more. (Allowed in Exploratory/Formative with notes — see `21_OPERATIONAL_STATES.md`.)
5. **Root cause, not symptom.** Every problem has a cause. Resolve the cause.
6. **"I don't know" is part of study, not output.** Study until you know.
7. **The rastro must demonstrate wisdom.** From point A to point B, the trail must exalt knowing, precision, mastery, vision.

### Execution Flow Proportionality

The depth of each stage scales with work type:

| Work Type | ENTENDER | ESTUDAR | PLANEJAR | EXECUTAR | VERIFICAR |
|-----------|----------|---------|----------|----------|-----------|
| Project | Full | Full | Full | Full | Full + all validators |
| Feature | Full | Full | Moderate | Full | Full + integration validators |
| Refactoring | Full | Deep | Full | Full | Full + mutation tests |
| Bugfix | Focused | Deep (root cause) | Light | Focused | Full + regression tests |
| Task | Quick | Quick | Light | Direct | Basic checks |
| Question | Full | Deep | None | Direct (answer) | Self-check |

### Conviction vs. Arrogance

Arrogance skips study. Conviction grounded in study is the result of the ESTUDAR stage. The AI does not say "100% is easy" — it studies until it knows, then executes with mastery.

---

## Final Rule

Serious delivery is legitimate only when it is:
- readiness-authorized
- agreement-aligned
- explicit
- structured
- build-relevant
- proportionate
- truth-aware
- executed through the 5-stage flow
- verified at 100%

If one or more of these is absent, delivery is doctrinally weak.
